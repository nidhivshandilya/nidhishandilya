Teaching a machine to write like me

Every draft it hands me is competent and none of it is mine. The sentences are the right length, the structure is tidy, the transitions all show up on time. It reads like a person who has never been annoyed by anything.

The tell is not vocabulary. It is rhythm, and it is refusals. I do not use em dashes. I start sentences with And when the sentence has earned it. I stop a paragraph one beat before the point is fully made, because I would rather leave a reader an inch of room than close every loop myself. None of that is a style guide. It is closer to a set of things I will not do, and a model trained on everything cannot easily learn a refusal.

So I am building a skill that learns how a person writes by watching an ordinary working session instead of asking them to describe themselves. Asking does not work. When someone asks me to describe my writing I do not tell the truth, I describe the writer I would like to be: crisper, funnier, better read. The description is aspiration wearing a lab coat.

What you actually want is the edits. Not the finished piece, the moment where a sentence got cut, or a word got swapped for a plainer one, or a whole paragraph came out because it was showing off. Finished writing is the average of a hundred decisions. The decisions are where the voice lives, and they are almost never written down.

Where it is now: it picks up rhythm fairly quickly, sentence length, where I break, how I open. Opinions take much longer, and I am not sure yet whether that is a limitation or the correct outcome. There is a version of this that is genuinely useful, where I stop rewriting the same four paragraphs every time. There is a version that is a machine for producing more of me, which is the last thing the internet needs.

It is still forming. That is the honest status of most things I build, and I have stopped treating it as a problem.

This is a first draft, published while I am still working it out.
